# B1 — Explain and Show — Design Document

## Category Purpose


Help students explain something clearly for a real purpose, to a listener who genuinely needs the information.

At B1, explaining is not a display of memorised knowledge. It is a communicative act with a goal: someone doesn't know something, and the student's job is to make them understand it, help them do it, or help them choose between options.

Students should:
- explain clearly
- give reasons
- recommend methods
- organise information logically
- help another person understand something
- choose the best approach where appropriate

The explanation should always be adapted to the listener.

Students should think about what information is most useful for this particular person, rather than simply explaining everything they know.


## Design Rule

Every B1 Explain and Show activity must have a listener with a real need.

The student is not describing a process in the abstract. They are helping a specific person who is confused, stuck, choosing between options, or about to do something for the first time.

Following the Waffle Brain B1 Principle, these activities should not increase imagination — they should increase judgement. The student should rarely invent a fictional world. Instead they respond to a realistic situation that requires organising information, weighing options, and communicating a decision or a method clearly.

The underlying question for the student should be:

"How do I explain this so it actually helps this person?"

rather than

"What can I imagine or invent?"

Every activity should contain:
- a clear goal (what the listener needs to walk away with)
- a realistic context (who they are, why they're asking)
- a reason to organise or prioritise the explanation
- where appropriate, a choice between methods that must be justified


## Good Examples

- Explaining how to use a service to someone who has never used it before
- Recommending the best way to do something, with reasons
- Teaching someone a simple skill, step by step, with a clear reason for the order
- Explaining a process while also giving practical advice or warnings
- Comparing two methods and recommending one, based on the listener's situation

Good:

"A new colleague doesn't know how to book time off at your company. Explain the process and give one useful tip."

Good:

"A friend wants to learn to ride a bike but is nervous. Explain how you would teach them, step by step."

Good:

"Someone wants to send money to a friend abroad. There are two ways to do it. Explain both and recommend one."


## Avoid

- explaining for no reason (no listener, no purpose)
- memorised process descriptions ("Describe how to make tea")
- interview questions ("How do you make tea?")
- abstract lectures with no practical grounding
- imagination-heavy scenarios that ask students to invent a world rather than respond to a realistic one

Avoid:

"Explain how photosynthesis works."

Avoid:

"Describe the process of making a sandwich."

These fail because there is no listener, no real need, and no decision to justify — they are closer to a vocabulary drill than a B1 speaking task.


## Language Focus

Students should naturally use:

- First,... / Then,... / After that,... / Finally,...
- The best way to... is...
- I'd recommend... / I would suggest...
- Make sure you... / Don't forget to...
- If you..., then...
- It's important to... because...
- One thing to be careful of is...
- Compared to [option], [option] is better because...
- The main advantage/disadvantage of... is...


## Prompt Structure

A strong B1 Explain and Show prompt has three parts:

1. **The listener** — who they are and why they need this explained (a colleague, a friend, a tourist, a family member, a new employee)
2. **The task** — what exactly the student must explain, teach, or recommend
3. **The extra demand** — a reason to organise, prioritise, compare, or justify (a tip, a warning, a choice between two methods, an order of steps)

Template shape:

"[Listener] needs to [do something] but [doesn't know how / is unsure / has two options]. Explain [what], and [give a reason / recommend one / add a tip]."


## Quality Check

Before a prompt is accepted, check:

- [ ] Is there a real listener with a genuine need?
- [ ] Does the student have to organise information, not just recite it?
- [ ] Is there a reason to explain (help, teach, recommend, warn) rather than just describe?
- [ ] Does it avoid requiring invented fictional worlds?
- [ ] Would a student naturally use B1 language (recommend, should, if...then, the best way to)?
- [ ] Could this be mistaken for an interview question? If yes, rewrite it as a task.
- [ ] Does it match the B1 golden rule: the student is communicating *in order to* do something, not simply to answer?
