# Waffle Brain A1/A2 Beginner Prompts

# Category: Describe

## Category Purpose

Describe helps beginners organise simple information clearly.

Students should describe real things they can easily imagine or see.

The goal is not perfect description.

The goal is confident speaking using familiar vocabulary.

---

## Design Rule

Every prompt should:

- focus on one clear object, place or person
- reduce cognitive load
- give students a structure to follow
- encourage complete sentences
- provide useful vocabulary naturally

Students should never wonder:

"What am I supposed to describe?"

---

## Good examples

Good:

"Describe your bedroom.

Talk about:
• furniture
• colours
• your favourite thing"

Good:

"Describe your school bag.

Talk about:
• what is inside
• what you use every day
• one unusual thing"

---

## Avoid

Avoid:

- Describe your personality
- Describe your perfect life
- Describe your dreams
- Describe happiness

These are abstract and belong to higher levels.

---

## Language Focus

Main language:

- There is...
- There are...
- It has...
- It is...
- I use...
- I like...
- I keep...

---

## Prompt Structure

Situation

↓

Object to describe

↓

3–4 talking points

↓

Teacher extension questions

---

## Quality Check

Before writing a prompt ask:

Can the student picture it immediately?

Will they know enough vocabulary?

Can the teacher easily ask follow-up questions?

## Activity Templates

Every Describe prompt should use one of these templates.

### Template 1 — Guided Tour

The student shows or imagines one place.

Structure:

Describe:

• what is there
• where things are
• what they like
• what they use

---

### Template 2 — Inside Something

The student describes what is inside an object.

Examples:

bag

fridge

desk

car

cupboard

---

### Template 3 — Compare Two Things

The student compares two familiar objects or places.

Examples:

old bedroom vs new bedroom

summer clothes vs winter clothes

home vs school

---

### Template 4 — Favourite Item

The student chooses one object and explains it.

Describe:

what it looks like

where they got it

why they like it

when they use it

---

### Template 5 — Spot the Difference

The teacher asks small follow-up questions that make the student notice extra details.

Examples:

"What colour is it?"

"What is next to it?"

"What do you keep there?"

"What would you change?"

---

### Template 6 — Teacher Guessing Game

The student describes something without saying its name.

The teacher tries to guess.

This naturally creates follow-up questions.
