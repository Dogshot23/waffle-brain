# Waffle Brain Level Design System

## Core Principle

The difference between levels is not only vocabulary.

Each level changes:
- the type of thinking required
- the amount of support provided
- the complexity of the interaction
- the grammar students naturally need


## A1/A2 Beginner

### Student Ability

Students can:
- describe familiar things
- explain simple routines
- make basic choices
- give simple reasons
- complete structured tasks

They need support to speak for longer periods.


### Prompt Style

Good prompts:
- use real situations
- have a clear goal
- provide structure
- reduce vocabulary searching
- allow multiple possible answers

Strong activity types:
- small problems
- planning tasks
- guided explanations
- simple decisions
- practical roleplays


### Avoid

Avoid:
- abstract opinions
- debates
- philosophical questions
- complex imagination
- "If you could..." questions
- open storytelling without support


### Thinking Tasks

Good:
"You are choosing food for a picnic. Help decide what to bring."

Less suitable:
"What is the best way to live a happy life?"


# B1 Intermediate

## Student Ability

Students can:
- explain opinions
- compare ideas
- describe experiences
- give reasons
- discuss advantages and disadvantages


## Prompt Style

Prompts can include:
- problems with more than one solution
- personal experiences
- opinions
- comparisons
- decision making


## Avoid

Avoid:
- simple vocabulary drills
- activities with only one correct answer
- purely factual questions


## Thinking Tasks

Good:
"Your town wants to improve tourism. Suggest three changes and explain why."

Less suitable:
"Choose your favourite restaurant."


# B2+ Advanced

## Student Ability

Students can:
- analyse ideas
- defend opinions
- evaluate arguments
- speculate
- discuss abstract topics


## Prompt Style

Prompts can include:
- debate
- ethical questions
- hypothetical situations
- complex social issues
- persuasion


## Avoid

Avoid:
- overly simple personal questions
- activities with no challenge


## Thinking Tasks

Good:
"Should governments limit social media use for teenagers? Discuss both sides."

Less suitable:
"Describe your morning routine."


# Level Boundaries

A1/A2:
Concrete → supported → practical

B1:
Personal → analytical → opinion based

B2+:
Abstract → critical → speculative


A prompt should move levels by changing the thinking required, not just by adding harder words.

Waffle Brain is a speaking activity generator for ESL teachers.

The goal is not to generate more questions. The goal is to create interesting speaking tasks that make students talk naturally.

Each level should feel different because the student is doing a different type of thinking, not just using easier vocabulary.

# A1/A2 Beginner

## Main goal

Help students produce simple spoken English confidently.

Students should talk about:
- themselves
- their daily life
- things they can see
- familiar situations

## Categories

### Everyday Situations

Purpose:
Help students talk about familiar real-life topics.

Focus:
- routines
- home
- food
- travel
- hobbies
- daily activities


### Describe

Purpose:
Build vocabulary and longer answers.

Focus:
- objects
- people
- places
- pictures
- situations


### Explain and Show

Purpose:
Practice explaining simple processes and actions.

Focus:
- instructions
- routines
- skills
- simple how-to activities


### Simple Roleplay

Purpose:
Practice real communication.

Focus:
- shops
- restaurants
- travel
- asking for help
- everyday problems


### Choose and Create

Purpose:
Help students make choices and expand answers.

Focus:
- selecting options
- creating plans
- designing simple ideas


### Guided Stories

Purpose:
Practice simple storytelling with support.

Focus:
- structured events
- sequences
- personal experiences with prompts

---

## Beginner rules

Allowed:
- personal experience
- simple description
- simple choices
- everyday situations
- short stories

Avoid:
- debates
- abstract ideas
- philosophy
- politics
- complex moral questions
- "argue both sides"

---

# B1 (Intermediate)

## Core Goal

At B1, students move beyond describing and begin making decisions, solving problems and explaining their reasoning.

The focus is not on using more difficult vocabulary.

The focus is on better thinking.

Students should regularly:

- compare options
- make recommendations
- explain choices
- solve everyday problems
- justify opinions
- prioritise information
- negotiate simple decisions

---

## Waffle Brain Principle

B1 does NOT increase imagination.

Instead it increases judgement.

Students should rarely need to invent complicated fictional situations.

Instead they should respond to realistic situations that require thought.

Good:

You have enough money for ONE improvement to your home.

Choose one.

Explain why.

Bad:

Imagine you are living on Mars...

---

## Design Rules

Every activity should contain:

- a clear goal
- realistic context
- limited choices or constraints
- a reason to justify decisions

Students should think:

"What would I do?"

rather than

"What imaginary story can I invent?"

---

## Language Focus

Students should naturally use:

- because
- although
- if
- should
- could
- might
- I would recommend...
- In my opinion...
- The best option is...
- On the other hand...

### Golden Rule

Every B1 activity should have a purpose.

Students should rarely speak simply to describe or give an opinion.

Instead they should be communicating in order to:

- solve a problem
- make a recommendation
- explain a decision
- compare alternatives
- improve something
- persuade someone
- help another person
---

# B2+ Advanced

## Main goal

Students analyse ideas and communicate complex opinions.

Students should:
- justify arguments
- consider different viewpoints
- discuss abstract topics

## Categories

### Debate

Arguments and opposing views.

### Society

Social issues and trends.

### Ethics

Moral questions and dilemmas.

### Future

Predictions and change.

### Creative Challenges

Unusual thinking tasks.

### Impossible Situations

Complex hypotheticals.

---

## Advanced rules

Allowed:
- abstract ideas
- debate
- analysis
- persuasion
- multiple viewpoints
- hypothetical situations

Avoid:
- simple fact questions
- basic personal questions only
- tasks requiring no reasoning

---

# General Waffle Brain Rule

Every prompt should feel like an activity, not an interview question.

Avoid:

"What is your favourite food?"

Prefer:

"You are creating a menu for a restaurant. Choose three dishes and explain why."

The student should have something to do, not just something to answer.
