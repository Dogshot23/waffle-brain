# B1 Simple Roleplay — Design Document

## Category Purpose

At B1, communication should normally have a purpose.

The student is trying to achieve something.

Most prompts should contain a realistic problem, disagreement or decision that requires communication to resolve.

Help students practise realistic spoken interaction where they must think, not just perform.

At B1, roleplay is not acting. It is not pretending to be someone else in an invented scenario. It is placing the student in a realistic situation where they must use judgement: negotiate, recommend, persuade politely, solve a problem, or make a decision with another person (real or simulated).

The student should stay recognisably themselves, dealing with a real-life type of situation, rather than stepping into a fictional character or imagined world.

This matches the B1 principle from the level design system: B1 does not increase imagination. It increases judgement.


## Design Rule

Every B1 Simple Roleplay prompt must:

- present a realistic, everyday interaction (not a fictional or invented world)
- give the student a clear goal within that interaction
- require a decision, negotiation, recommendation, or solution — not just a description or a performance
- include a constraint or limitation that forces judgement (limited money, limited time, competing options, a disagreement to resolve)
- give the student a reason to justify their choice or position

The test for every prompt:

Does the student have to think, decide, and justify — or are they just play-acting a scene?

If the situation could be resolved by the student simply reciting a memorised script (ordering food, asking for directions) without any real decision or reasoning, it belongs at A1/A2, not B1.

If the situation requires the student to invent a fictional identity, world, or backstory, it does not belong in this category.


## Good Examples

(For calibration only — illustrating the type of situation, not prompt wording.)

- A student and a shop assistant disagree about a return policy and must reach a solution.
- A tenant needs to persuade a landlord to fix a problem, working within a limited budget or timeframe.
- Two people planning a shared trip have different priorities and must agree on a compromise.
- A customer wants a service changed or improved and must negotiate terms with the provider.
- A student must recommend one option to a friend from a small set of realistic alternatives, and defend that recommendation when the friend disagrees.

Each of these situations is grounded in real life, has a concrete goal, and cannot be resolved without the student reasoning through a choice.


## Avoid

- fictional characters, invented identities, or costumes ("You are a pirate...", "You are a superhero...")
- fantastical or impossible settings (space travel, magic, imaginary worlds)
- scenarios with only one correct response (ordering a coffee, asking the time)
- scenarios that are purely transactional with no disagreement, choice, or constraint
- interview-style prompts disguised as roleplay ("Tell me about a time you...")
- prompts requiring heavy invention of backstory before the student can even begin
- abstract or philosophical framing (this stays at B2+)
- scenarios with no realistic stakes or consequence to the decision


## Language Focus

Roleplay situations at this level should naturally draw out:

- negotiation language: "Would it be possible to...", "What if we...", "I was hoping we could..."
- polite persuasion: "I understand, but...", "Could I suggest...", "It would really help if..."
- recommending and advising: "I'd recommend...", "Why don't you...", "It might be better to..."
- justifying a position: "because", "since", "the reason is..."
- comparing options: "rather than", "instead of", "on the other hand"
- softened disagreement: "I see your point, but...", "That's fair, although..."
- conditional reasoning: "if", "would", "could", "might"


## Prompt Structure

Each prompt should establish:

1. **Situation** — a realistic, everyday context involving another party (a service provider, a friend, a colleague, a stranger with a shared problem)
2. **Goal** — what the student needs to achieve by the end of the interaction
3. **Constraint** — a limitation that prevents an easy or obvious answer (budget, time, competing needs, a prior disagreement)
4. **Justification requirement** — an implicit or explicit need to explain or defend the choice made

Prompts should read as a real task the student needs to handle, not as a stage direction for a performance.


## Quality Check

Before a B1 Simple Roleplay prompt is accepted, confirm:

- [ ] Is the situation something that could genuinely happen in real life?
- [ ] Does the student have to negotiate, persuade, recommend, decide, or solve — not just describe or perform?
- [ ] Is there a real constraint that makes the outcome non-obvious?
- [ ] Does the student need to justify their choice or position at some point?
- [ ] Is the student staying as themselves, not adopting a fictional character?
- [ ] Could a B1 student attempt this without inventing a large amount of backstory first?
- [ ] Does the prompt avoid being answerable with a single memorised phrase?
- [ ] Does the prompt avoid abstract, philosophical, or purely hypothetical framing (reserved for B2+)?

If any answer is no, the prompt should be revised or removed.
