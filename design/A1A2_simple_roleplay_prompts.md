# Waffle Brain A1/A2 Simple Roleplay Prompts

## Category Purpose

Simple Roleplay creates realistic speaking situations where the student has a clear role, goal, and reason to communicate.

The purpose is not to perform a long dramatic roleplay.

The purpose is to give beginners a safe situation where they need to use practical English.

---

## Design Rule

Every roleplay should include:

- a clear situation
- two simple roles
- a reason to communicate
- a small problem, decision, or goal

The student should have something to achieve.

---

## Good Roleplay Examples

Good:

"You are ordering food at a restaurant. The teacher is the waiter. Decide what you want and ask questions."

"You are moving house. Ask your friend for help choosing what to take."

"You are buying clothes. Explain what you need."

---

## Avoid

Avoid:

- long imaginary stories
- complicated characters
- pretending to be famous people
- situations requiring advanced vocabulary
- roleplays with no clear goal

Avoid:

"Imagine you are a millionaire. Explain your lifestyle."

This creates imagination problems, not speaking practice.

---

## Language Focus

Mainly:

- present simple
- present continuous
- going to future
- simple requests
- basic questions

Useful language:

"I need..."
"I want..."
"Can I...?"
"Could you...?"
"Do you have...?"
"I think..."

---

## Prompt Structure

Each prompt should follow:

Situation

↓

Teacher setup

↓

Student goal

↓

Choices or useful language support

↓

Extension questions

---

## Quality Check

Before adding a prompt:

Ask:

Can a beginner understand the situation quickly?

Does the student know what they need to do?

Will this create more than one-word answers?

Could a Cambly teacher use this immediately?

# Review

## Category Check

Does this category create real communication?

Yes.

Students have:
- a role
- a goal
- a reason to speak
- choices to reduce cognitive load

---

## Prompt Check

Every prompt should:

✓ be usable immediately by a teacher  
✓ avoid interview style questions  
✓ give beginners enough language support  
✓ create more than one-word answers  
✓ use familiar situations  
✓ allow follow-up questions  

---

## Language Level Check

Main language:

- present simple
- simple requests
- basic questions
- simple future plans

Avoid:

- complex roleplay characters
- abstract situations
- advanced negotiation
- long storytelling
