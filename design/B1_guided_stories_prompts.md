# B1 Guided Stories — Design Document

## Category Purpose

Guided Stories at B1 help students build realistic stories around problems, decisions and consequences.

Students should:
- organise events logically
- explain decisions made by the characters
- describe causes and consequences
- solve problems within the story
- reflect on why events happened

This is not a creative writing category. It is a judgement and reasoning category told through narrative form. The story is a vehicle for decision-making and cause-and-effect thinking, not an exercise in imagination.


## Design Rule

Following the Waffle Brain B1 Principle, this category does NOT increase imagination — it increases judgement.

Every story must be built around a realistic problem or decision, not an invented fictional world.

Students should think:

"What would happen next, and why?"

rather than

"What imaginary story can I invent?"

Every guided story must contain:
- a clear beginning (realistic situation)
- a problem or decision
- an outcome
- a reason why things happened

If a prompt can be answered without the student explaining a decision or a consequence, it does not belong in this category.


## Good Examples

- a journey where something unexpected happens
- solving a misunderstanding
- making an important decision
- dealing with a small everyday problem
- helping someone during an event

These work because they place the student inside a realistic sequence of events that requires organising, deciding, and explaining — not inventing from nothing.


## Avoid

Avoid:
- fantasy
- science fiction
- magical events
- open-ended creative writing
- "Tell me about a time..."
- stories with no purpose or conflict

"Tell me about a time..." is excluded even though it sounds like a story prompt, because it relies on the student already having a personal memory to retrieve, rather than giving them a structured problem to reason through. It also has no guaranteed conflict or decision point.

Open-ended creative writing is excluded because it removes constraints — B1 stories should feel like realistic situations the student is guided through, not free invention.


## Language Focus

Students should naturally use:

- past simple / past continuous (for sequencing events)
- because / so / as a result
- although / but
- when / after / before / as soon as
- had to / decided to / ended up
- I think the reason was...
- In the end...
- If I had known... (light, occasional — not the focus)

The emphasis stays on sequencing and cause-effect language, not on complex hypothetical or speculative grammar — that belongs at B2+.


## Prompt Structure

Each Guided Stories prompt should follow this shape:

1. **Situation** — a short, realistic starting point (who, where, what's happening)
2. **Turning point** — a problem, unexpected event, or decision that must be made
3. **Task** — a clear instruction telling the student what to do with the story (explain the decision, describe what happened next, explain the outcome)
4. **Implicit requirement** — the student must supply a reason, not just a sequence of events

Example shape (structure only, not a finished prompt):

> You are [realistic role/situation]. [Something happens that creates a problem or requires a decision]. Tell the story: what happened, what you decided to do, and why.


## Quality Check

Before a prompt is accepted into this category, confirm:

- [ ] Is the situation realistic (no fantasy, sci-fi, or magic)?
- [ ] Does the story contain a genuine problem or decision, not just an event?
- [ ] Does the task require the student to explain a decision or a consequence?
- [ ] Is there a clear beginning, problem/decision, and outcome?
- [ ] Does the prompt avoid relying on the student recalling a real personal memory ("Tell me about a time...")?
- [ ] Could the student reasonably invent realistic details without needing complex imagination?
- [ ] Does the prompt encourage past-tense sequencing and cause-effect language rather than opinion or debate language (that's B1 Decisions/Problem-Solving territory) or abstract speculation (that's B2+)?

If a prompt fails any of these checks, it should be revised or moved to a different category.
