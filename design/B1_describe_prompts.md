# B1 — Describe

## Category Purpose

At A1/A2, Describe is about building vocabulary and longer answers — naming and describing what is there.

At B1, Describe moves beyond naming. The purpose is to describe something real while also making a judgement about it.

Students should not just report what something looks like. They should:

- compare it to something else
- identify what matters most about it
- explain why it works or doesn't work
- notice how it has changed
- organise the description logically, not as a list of features

The description is the vehicle. The thinking is the goal.


## Design Rule

Every B1 Describe prompt should give the student something real to look at or think about, and a reason to evaluate it.

Where possible, prompts should contain a purpose for the description.

The student should usually be describing something because they are making a recommendation, solving a problem, identifying an improvement, or helping someone make a decision.

Students should be answering:

"What is this like, and what do I think about it?"

not

"What is this?"

A good B1 Describe prompt always contains two layers:

1. A concrete thing to describe (a place, a room, a photo, a product, a change)
2. A judgement to make about it (better/worse, effective/ineffective, before/after, more/less suitable)

If a prompt only asks the student to describe features with no comparison or evaluation, it belongs at A1/A2, not B1.


## Good Examples

Compare two places:

"Compare the café where you usually study with a library. Which one helps you concentrate more, and why?"

Describe improvements:

"Describe your kitchen. What is one change that would make it more practical?"

Evaluate a workspace:

"Describe your desk or workspace. Is it well organised for working? What would you change?"

Explain why a design works better:

"Look at two logos for a coffee shop. Which one is more effective, and why?"

Compare photographs:

"Compare these two photos of the same street. Which one looks more welcoming to visitors?"

Describe changes over time:

"Describe how your neighbourhood has changed in the last few years. Is it better or worse now?"


## Avoid

- Simple object description ("Describe your bedroom.") — no comparison, no judgement, this is A1/A2.
- Vocabulary listing ("Describe what you can see in this picture.") — turns into naming items, not evaluating them.
- Interview questions ("What does your house look like?") — invites a flat answer with no built-in task.
- Abstract analysis ("What makes design beautiful?") — no concrete anchor; this belongs at B2+.
- Imagination-heavy scenarios ("Describe your dream house.") — invented rather than real, reduces judgement to fantasy.

The situation should always stay grounded in something real or realistically comparable — a place the student knows, a photo they can look at, a change they've actually seen.


## Language Focus

Prompts in this category should naturally pull students toward:

- more ... than / less ... than
- compared to
- the main difference is...
- what stands out is...
- it works well because...
- it doesn't work because...
- it used to be... but now...
- one thing that would improve it is...
- overall, I think...


## Prompt Structure

Each prompt should follow this shape:

1. Give the student something concrete to observe or recall (a place, object, photo, room, situation).
2. Ask for a comparison, evaluation, or noticed change — not just a description.
3. Ask for a reason ("why", "what would you change", "which is better").

Structure template:

"[Concrete thing/s to describe]. [Comparison or judgement question]. [Reason prompt]."


## Quality Check

Before finalising a prompt, check:

- Does it require the student to compare, evaluate, or notice change — not just list features?
- Is there something concrete and real (or realistically comparable) to anchor the description?
- Does it ask "why" or "which is better", not just "what is it like"?
- Could a student answer it with a flat list of adjectives? If yes, rewrite it.
- Is it free of invented/fantasy scenarios?
- Is it free of abstract, B2+-level analysis with no concrete object?
- Does it feel like a small evaluation task, not an interview question?
